Four things this run says about the portfolio

The replay produces seven frames and seven non-blocking gates. Read as investment output rather than as diagnostics, they converge on four statements — each one measured on this run, each one checkable in the tables below.

Concentration

It is a 12-stock portfolio wearing a 50-stock label

Eight names sit at the 10% position cap and hold 80% of capital. The bottom thirty-eight hold 1.17% between them — the smallest is 0.0002%, which is not a position, it is a number that survived a sort. Effective N is 11.7.

Anything said about “the fifty names” is really a statement about eight.

Style

An unhedged small-cap bet nobody asked for

85.9% small cap against a 51.6% universe share, and 0.7% large cap against 21.5%. Core style takes 83.7% against 56.7%. None of these is a stated objective; all three fall out of the ranking.

The model's own size posture agrees only in the weakest sense: Small Cap is NEUTRAL at +2.16pp on a ±2.53pp band, while Large and Mid Cap are both formal underweights.

Selection

It buys where consensus is loudest

Book median analyst upside is +54.6% against a universe median of +18.1%; the eight capped positions run from +85% to +144%. The model's own expected upside ranks the universe at Spearman 0.992 against raw consensus.

The portfolio is therefore a leveraged expression of the analyst panel's most extreme targets — which is precisely the region where a price target carries the least information.

Risk

The downside it optimised against does not exist

Every one of the fifty names has a positive 95% VaR: the modelled 1-in-20 bad year is a gain of +23.2% at the median name. The worst single scenario out of two thousand loses 0.84%.

The ranking denominator that produced this book is 21.7× smaller at its largest book value than the universe median. Ranking on reward-per-risk selected on the absence of modelled risk.

Every number here scores the model against the analyst trail it was fitted to. No internal check can tell a favourable modelled tail from an optimistic one — which is why none of this moves a default, and why the vintage capture remains the only thing that would settle it.

A full-weight position on a panel of two

The size-down watch flags 325 names on this run — every one of them for thin coverage (n_analysts = 2), none for a wide posterior. Three reached the book, and one of them, EOLUB, is at the full 10% cap.

The veto is reported, not applied. Turning it on removes 10% of the portfolio and re-spills it, so it is a decision to take deliberately rather than by omission: --size-down-veto.

§3

Does the book express the model's views?

The same posterior produces two independent things: a ranked book, and a group posture — over- and underweights by sector, country, size and style, each shrunk by λ = τ²/(τ²+s²) so that a poorly-resolved group is pulled toward zero rather than to an extreme. Nothing forces them to agree. This run is the first where they have been laid side by side.

The concentration is endorsed — barely

Health Care is 33.2% of the book, +22.1pp active, and it is the run's only OVERWEIGHT sector. But it clears the band by four hundredths of a point: +2.68pp against ±2.63pp.

So the book's largest bet is endorsed by a signal that would have been silent on a marginally different draw. Read it as concurrence, not as conviction — and note that Industrials at +11.4pp active carries no endorsement at all.

It holds 10% of the sector it likes least

Financials is the model's strongest sector call in either direction — UNDERWEIGHT at −6.39pp, resolved at λ = 0.64 across 939 names. The book holds Hypoport SE at the full cap regardless.

A single name overriding a well-resolved sector view is defensible — that is what stock selection is — but it should be a stated exception, and here it is a side effect. The posture layer and the ranking layer never consult each other.

§4

Why these names, mechanically

The shipped ranking is expected_return / downside_dev. Two measurements on this run explain the entire book, and they compound.

The two halves of the ratio are negatively correlated

Across the universe, downside_dev correlates −0.68 (Spearman) with expected_return. The names with the biggest modelled upside are, by construction of the forward simulation, the names whose downside deviation has collapsed toward zero.

So the ratio does not trade reward against risk. It multiplies a high numerator by a vanishing denominator and ranks on the product — which is why the top of the ranking is a plateau and the cut lands on a tie.

The floor admits the band it exists to exclude

The absolute floor removed the denominators that were exactly zero and stopped there. The problem is the band just above it, which the floor admits and the ranking then rewards. On this run 59.2% of the universe has tail_risk sitting on its volatility floor and 10.2% has a floored downside_dev.

A relative floor — a fraction of the eligible universe's median, which excludes rather than clamps — ships alongside it at q = 0.0, off by default so the shipped arm stays bit-identical.

The model's own departure from consensus is real but small: the shrinkage_slope gate measures slope 0.834, median revision 1.02pp, and rank correlation 0.99245 against a ceiling of 0.995. It passes — but a screen that reproduces the consensus ordering at ρ 0.992 is adding risk structure, not a different view of value. That structure is what the book is selected on, and it is the part nothing has validated.

§5

A portfolio that cannot lose money

Three generative tail statistics per name, all read off the same two thousand forward scenarios. They are ordered GVaR ≥ GES ≥ GTR by construction, so their position relative to zero is the whole claim.

50 of 50

book names whose modelled 1-in-20 outcome is a gain. Weighted across the portfolio the 95% VaR is +40.6% and the expected shortfall is +29.7%. Universe-wide, 49.9% of all 6,518 names carry a positive GVaR — the book is not an outlier in a healthy universe, it is the tail of a universe that is half like this.

Sizing

Kelly is a pin, not a size

89.6% of the universe carries kelly_fraction = 1.0 exactly, and 0 of 50 book names have an interior solution. A pin is the bisection reporting that E[log(1+f·r)] never turned over inside the feasible interval — no simulated scenario loses money.

A further 9.7% of the universe is formally unbounded: the fraction has no finite maximum at all. Those rows export as NULL with a boolean saying why, never as an infinity.

Diversification

Fifty names, twelve names of risk

Herfindahl concentration puts the book's effective N at 11.7. With factor_share = 0.35, shared market / region / sector blocks take 35% of shock variance and leave an idiosyncratic weight of 0.806 — so pooling averages most of the name-specific risk away, and what survives is the 35% that does not diversify.

That split is a prior, not an estimate. It cannot move a per-name marginal — the variance split is preserving — but it decides the joint distribution the book is sized against, which is exactly the quantity a twelve-name portfolio is most sensitive to.

Actions

An action list that mostly says yes

5,436 BUY · 1,004 HOLD · 73 AVOID — 83.5% of the universe is a BUY, and all fifty book names are. Read alongside the risk ladder this is one finding, not two.

A forward simulation in which half the universe cannot lose money will produce an action list that rarely says no. The gates on the fit are all clean; they simply do not measure this.

§6

What to do with this book

Ranked by how much each changes the portfolio for how little it costs to find out. None of them moves a shipped default; all of them are replay flags against the fit that already exists.

1 · Run the other two ranking arms before trading any of it

One posterior, three denominators. reward_to_cvar collapses to its volatility floor; p_upside_pos_cond is bounded, so it cannot be inflated by a vanishing denominator at all — it saturates instead, which is a failure mode you can see. This run ranked on one arm and exported one book, so portfolio_book_agreement never fired.

If the three arms share most of their names, the concentration above is a property of the posterior. If they share few, it is a property of the ratio — and the book is an artifact of a formula choice nobody has defended in writing.

cost seconds · --rank-arms all

2 · Put a cap on Health Care and re-spill

33.2% in one sector, 30% of it in three small-cap therapeutics, endorsed by a group signal that clears its band by 0.04pp. The sizing rule has a sector_cap parameter and this run set it to None.

The gate exists precisely so that no cap is a choice someone made rather than a line nobody wrote. At 30% the spill would push weight into Industrials, which the model rates NEUTRAL — so this trades an endorsed concentration for an unendorsed one. Worth measuring before deciding.

cost seconds · --sector-cap 0.30

3 · Decide the size-down veto explicitly

Three book names sit on an analyst panel of two, one of them at the full cap. The veto is orthogonal to the ranking: thin coverage is exactly the condition that produces a forward simulation with no credible left tail, which is what the ranking is selecting on. Applying it removes 10% of the portfolio.

cost seconds · --size-down-veto

4 · Sweep the two priors that set the whole departure from consensus

forecast_error_multiplier buys the entire departure from analyst consensus — at κ = 0 the screen reproduces it at ρ 0.999995. factor_share is variance-preserving, so it cannot move a per-name marginal but decides the joint distribution the book is sized against.

Neither is identified by the panel. Both are chosen numbers, and the sweep is the only honest way to report how much of the answer depends on them.

cost ~1 min · --sweep factor_share,multiplier

5 · Capture the vintage — still the only thing that would settle any of it

Every measurement on this page scores the model against the analyst trail it was fitted to. A book whose modelled 1-in-20 is +40.6% is not a book any internal check can approve or reject. The handoff now carries run_id, source_sha and source_dirty, which is what makes a later realised-return comparison attributable to the code that produced it.

cost one quarter of waiting · scripts/capture_panel_vintage.py

Reproducing this

The v2 workflow writes a compact handoff after the screen — four posterior quantities the forward simulation actually reads, not the raw InferenceData. Every figure and table above comes from replaying that file; no sampling, no database write.

# the run above, exactly as it was produced
python kalman_portfolio.py --handoff pymc_kalman_filter_pt_v2_results/07_posterior/07_forecast_handoff_v2.nc

# the two gates this replay did not emit: three books, and both prior sweeps
python kalman_portfolio.py --rank-arms all --sweep factor_share,multiplier

# take the concentration and the veto deliberately rather than by omission
python kalman_portfolio.py --sector-cap 0.30 --size-down-veto

§8

Still open

Carried forward from the previous edition of this page, because none of it was closed by this run.

The vintage capture

Still the top item, and still not done. Every gate above is internal, and the forward tail both books select on stays unvalidated until realised returns exist. Run 807df55e7158 sharpens the case rather than answering it: a book whose modelled 1-in-20 is +40.6% is not a book any internal check can approve or reject. What the handoff's provenance stamping adds is the piece that makes a later vintage attributable to the code that produced it — and this run is the first with a clean source tree, so source_sha 5667681 fully determines what ran.

An overflow in the ergodicity curve

terminal_wealth_curve raises RuntimeWarning: overflow encountered in exp. Reproduced at book scale it returns +inf for 21 of 100 bet fractions: log_growth stays finite, but the terminal wealth it is exponentiated back into does not. It reaches no exported frame, so no gate sees it — which is exactly why it is written down. The same class of defect, in a column that was exported, refused an analytics write earlier this month.

Mean-model arms from a replay

Max-and-Smooth screens mean-structure arms in seconds against a frozen covariance, but it freezes exactly the parameters the forward simulation reads, and the handoff carries the four quantities the simulator needs rather than the model graph. The stage refuses rather than silently degrading into a comparison of one arm against itself.

Writing to the analytics schema

The replay exports CSV. The v2 tables are DROP-and-RECREATE, so a replay that wrote into them would destroy the export it was replaying. Given §2, precedence between the two books has to be declared in the v2 DDL before either can be the table the dashboard reads.
